Investigation & build plan

Room airflow & temperature simulator

A browser tool that answers one question: given an AC here, a fan there, and a door open, what temperature is each part of my room actually going to be? Below is a working 2D solver proving the physics runs in real time, then the plan for the 3D version.

01

What makes this hard, and what makes it tractable

Real CFD means solving Navier–Stokes with a turbulence model on a fine mesh, resolving boundary layers a millimetre thick. That is minutes-to-hours per case on a workstation, and it is not what you need.

What you need is directionally correct and instantly responsive — drag the fan to the other window and watch the cold layer either sweep across the room or short-circuit straight out. That is a coarse grid, a stable incompressible solver, and buoyancy. Which is very cheap.

03

The model, honestly scoped

Simulated
  • Incompressible bulk airflow — jets, recirculation, dead zones
  • Buoyancy from real density difference (hot air rises, AC air falls)
  • Temperature carried by the flow, plus turbulent mixing
  • Momentum and thermal sources: AC, fans, heaters, bodies, appliances
  • Open boundaries: doors, windows, frame gaps, door undercuts
  • Conduction through walls by U-value, and solar gain through glass
  • Thermal mass of walls and floor lagging behind the air
Not simulated
  • Turbulence models — mixing is a tuned eddy diffusivity, not resolved eddies
  • Boundary layers at wall scale; the first cell is inches thick, not millimetres
  • Humidity, latent heat, condensation (add later if you want real comfort indices)
  • Wind pressure on the building envelope driving stack effect
  • Radiant exchange between surfaces and occupants
  • Duct and refrigeration-cycle behaviour — the AC is a boundary condition, not a machine

The result should be read as a comparison engine, not a thermometer. “Configuration A leaves this corner 6° warmer than configuration B” is trustworthy. “That corner will be exactly 78.4°F” is not.

04

Solver architecture

One timestep
01
Apply sources
Prescribe velocity and temperature at AC outlets, fan faces, heat loads.
02
Buoyancy
Boussinesq: vertical acceleration g·ΔT/T₀ from local temperature deviation.
03
Advect velocity
Semi-Lagrangian backtrace with trilinear sampling. Unconditionally stable at any timestep.
04
Project to divergence-free
Poisson solve for pressure, then subtract its gradient. This is where the whole room becomes coupled — opening a door changes flow everywhere at once.
05
Advect + diffuse heat
Temperature rides the velocity field, plus an eddy-diffusivity term standing in for turbulent mixing.
06
Envelope exchange
Conduction into wall thermal mass by U-value; solar gain injected at glazing.
Boundary vocabulary
SOLID
Walls, furniture. No flow through; free-slip along. Carries thermal mass.
INLET
AC or supply register. Fixed velocity vector plus fixed supply temperature.
OUTLET
Exhaust fan. Fixed outward flux; temperature whatever arrives.
OPEN
Doorway, open window, frame gap. Zero-pressure boundary — the solver decides which way air goes. Inflow arrives at outside temperature.
SOURCE
Bodies, laptops, ovens, sunlit floor patch. Watts in, no momentum.

OPEN is the piece that makes leakage questions answerable — you never tell it how much air comes in the door, only that the door is there.

05

Going 3D

Rendering the invisible

Air has no surface, so the whole design problem is making a scalar field legible in a perspective view. Four layers, independently toggleable:

  • Raymarched volume — temperature as translucent coloured haze, one 3D texture, one fullscreen shader pass
  • Slice planes — draggable horizontal and vertical cuts, sharp and readable, best for actual numbers
  • Particle tracers — a few thousand points seeded in the flow, streaked and tinted by local temperature; this is what makes airflow readable
  • Isosurfaces — a hull around “below 74°” to see the cooled envelope as a solid shape
Interaction model

The room is defined in a 2D plan view — drag walls to size it, drop windows and doors onto wall segments, place devices on the floor plan. Then flip to 3D to read results. Plan view for authoring, 3D for understanding.

Devices snap to the surfaces they belong on: an AC to a window, a ceiling fan to the ceiling, a floor fan anywhere with a rotatable aim cone. Everything is live — nudge the fan and the field re-settles in a couple of seconds rather than re-running a job.

Probes are the payoff: drop a thermometer anywhere, get a live number and a history graph, and compare two configurations side by side.
